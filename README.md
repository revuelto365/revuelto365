## Know Your Customer (KYC)

### Who we trade with

Revuelto's supplied KYC policy describes identity checks intended to establish who holds an account, help keep client funds separate, direct withdrawals to the appropriate account holder, and prevent accounts from being opened under another person's identity.

#### 01 — Why verification is required

The policy states that the company establishes and records each client's identity before the account trades and keeps those records current. It also describes verification as a safeguard against unauthorized access and misuse of account details.

#### 02 — What documents may be requested

The supplied policy identifies two document categories:

| Document type | Examples / information |
| --- | --- |
| Government-issued photo ID | Passport, national identity card, or driving licence showing full name, date of birth, and expiry date |
| Proof of current address | Utility bill, bank statement, or council tax notice dated within the previous three months and showing the client's name and current address |

#### 03 — When verification may take place

According to the supplied policy, verification may be requested:

- When an account is opened.
- Before the first withdrawal.
- When a document expires or a client's circumstances change.
- When account activity changes materially from the activity expected at account opening.

#### 04 — Document handling

The policy states that documents are encrypted in transit and at rest, access is restricted to staff whose roles require it, and access is logged. It further states that records are retained for the legally required period after account closure and then destroyed.

#### 05 — Enhanced due diligence

Additional checks may apply to politically exposed persons, clients in higher-risk jurisdictions, or accounts with unusually structured funding. The policy says these checks may include questions about source of funds and source of wealth, and that the company may decline to proceed.

#### 06 — Client rights

The supplied policy says clients may ask what personal information is held, request correction of inaccurate information, and ask why a verification decision was made. It also notes that legal requirements may limit when certain decisions or information can be changed or disclosed.

## Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

### Money with a traceable past

Revuelto's supplied AML policy describes controls intended to identify the origin of funds, monitor account activity, screen clients, and report unresolved suspicions to relevant authorities.

#### 01 — Our commitment

The policy states that funds whose origin cannot be established will not be accepted for trading and may be returned to their source. It also says the company may refuse to open or may close an account that does not satisfy these checks.

#### 02 — A risk-based approach

The supplied policy describes assessing accounts using factors such as:

- Jurisdiction.
- Funding method.
- Expected account activity.
- Client profile.

It states that the level of scrutiny is proportionate to the assessed risk.

#### 03 — Monitoring account activity

The policy identifies examples of activity that may receive additional attention:

- Funds deposited and withdrawn without corresponding trading activity.
- Deposits structured to remain below applicable thresholds.
- Payment instruments held in a name different from the account holder's.
- Significant changes in account behaviour without an adequate explanation.

#### 04 — Sanctions and screening

The supplied policy states that clients are screened against applicable sanctions and watch lists during onboarding and on an ongoing basis. It also says screening considers beneficial owners, not only the account name, and that confirmed matches are frozen and reported.

#### 05 — Reporting suspicion

The policy says unresolved suspicions may be reported to the relevant authority. It also notes that applicable law may prohibit the company from informing an account holder that a report has been made.

#### 06 — Governance and training

The supplied material describes a designated officer responsible for the policy, staff training when employees join and annual refreshers, independent review of the programme, and retention of records for the statutory period.
